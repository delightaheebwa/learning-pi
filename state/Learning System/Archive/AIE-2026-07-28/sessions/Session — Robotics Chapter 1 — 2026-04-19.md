## What We Covered

> Summary of the session content.

- Reviewed the due spaced-repetition concepts first.
- Started the robotics chapter and defined robotics as the field that designs, builds, programs, and uses robots.
- Compared several robot definitions and pulled out the shared core features: reprogrammable, multifunctional, computer-controlled, sensing, acting, and autonomy.
- Explained degrees of freedom as the number of ways a robot can move and act.
- Separated control theory from cybernetics: control theory gives the math and logic for stable, precise behavior, while cybernetics focuses on feedback loops between sensing, acting, and environmental response.
- Established the three historical roots of robotics: control theory, cybernetics, and artificial intelligence.
- Covered why robots are useful: repetitive work, harsh conditions, consistency, and avoiding fatigue.
- Introduced the 3D’s: dirty, dull, and dangerous tasks.
- Explained optimization as improving a process toward goals such as efficiency, safety, comfort, and reliability.
- Reviewed the chapter’s myths and facts about robots.
- Covered Asimov’s laws of robotics and the hierarchy they create.
