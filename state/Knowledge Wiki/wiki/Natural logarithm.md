# Natural logarithm

The natural logarithm \(\ln(a)\) is the conversion factor that appears when expressing an exponential base \(a\) in terms of base \(e\):

- \(a^t = e^{\ln(a)t}\)

That identity immediately gives the derivative rule:

- \(\frac{d}{dt} a^t = \ln(a)\,a^t\)

## Interpretation

So \(\ln(a)\) tells you how fast the exponential grows relative to itself.

Examples:

- \(\ln(2) \approx 0.6931\)
- \(\ln(5) \approx 1.6094\)
- \(\ln(42) \approx 3.7377\)
