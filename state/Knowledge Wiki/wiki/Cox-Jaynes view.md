# Cox-Jaynes view

Cox-Jaynes says that if you represent degrees of plausibility with real numbers and demand rational consistency, you are essentially forced into the usual rules of probability.

## Core claim

- Plausibility values are real numbers.
- They must update in ways that agree with common sense.
- The algebra of plausibility must be consistent: the same information pattern should always produce the same numbers.
- Under those requirements, the rules that emerge are the standard probability rules, especially the sum rule and product rule, up to a monotone re-labeling of the scale.

## What consistency means here

- **Non-contradiction**: if two routes lead to the same conclusion, they must give the same plausibility.
- **Honesty**: all available data must be used.
- **Reproducibility**: the same state of knowledge must produce the same plausibility assignment.

## Why it matters

This makes probability look less like an arbitrary convention and more like the unique consistent language for plausible reasoning under uncertainty.

A monotone re-labeling is allowed, so you can work in another scale such as log-odds without changing the underlying logic.
