# Exponential derivatives

Exponential functions are special because their derivative is proportional to the function itself.

## Core rule

For any positive base \(a\) with \(a \neq 1\):

- \(\frac{d}{dt} a^t = \ln(a)\,a^t\)

That means differentiating an exponential does not change its shape; it only multiplies the function by a constant.

## Examples

- \(\frac{d}{dt} 2^t \approx 0.6931\,2^t\)
- \(\frac{d}{dt} 3^t \approx 1.0986\,3^t\)
- \(\frac{d}{dt} 5^t \approx 1.6094\,5^t\)
- \(\frac{d}{dt} 42^t \approx 3.7377\,42^t\)

## Why this matters

This is the bridge to [[Euler number e]]: if the proportionality constant is \(1\), then the function equals its own derivative.

It also connects to [[Natural logarithm]], since \(\ln(a)\) is exactly the constant that appears in the derivative.

A related applied model is [[Newtons law of cooling]], where the rate of change is proportional to the current distance from equilibrium.
