## Benchmark questions (verbatim, as supplied)

### Low — target 90%

1. What is the book's central argument about the relationship between money and psychology?
2. According to Housel, what is the key difference between how physics and finance work?
3. Why does Housel believe knowing what to do financially is not enough?
4. Which two fields does Housel argue help us understand financial behavior better than finance alone?
5. What are the three human qualities Housel mentions when explaining why people bury themselves in debt?

### Mid — target 80%

6. Why does Housel compare financial behavior to human behavior rather than the predictable laws of physics?
7. Why might two people have equally convincing but completely different explanations of the same financial event?
8. How does the example of investors selling during a bear market demonstrate the importance of psychology?
9. Why does Housel think studying interest rates alone cannot fully explain why people get into debt?
10. What does Housel mean when he suggests that financial knowledge tells us what to do, but not what happens in our heads when we try to do it?

### High — target 70%

11. Two university students receive the same monthly allowance. One saves consistently; the other spends almost everything. Using Housel's argument, explain why giving them identical financial advice might not produce identical results.
12. Imagine someone understands the mathematics of investing but panics and sells whenever the market falls. What does this reveal about the limits of financial knowledge?
13. How could studying someone's personal history help explain their financial decisions?
14. Housel argues that money offers a magnifying glass for understanding human behavior. Explain what he means and give an example beyond investing.
15. If you were teaching someone how to manage money, how would the introduction change the way you approach that task?
