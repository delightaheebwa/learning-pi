## Extracted notes

- In 1D, the derivative is the local scaling factor near a point.
- In 2D, the same idea becomes a linear map that best approximates the function near a point.
- That linear map is the Jacobian matrix.
- The Jacobian depends on the point where it is evaluated.
- Each column of the Jacobian tells how the output changes when one input coordinate changes a little.
- To compute it, freeze one variable and differentiate the resulting 1D slice.
- The first column comes from varying x while holding y fixed.
- The second column comes from varying y while holding x fixed.
- The Jacobian determinant measures how areas scale near the chosen point.
- This scaling factor is what later appears in change of variables for integrals.
