## Learning Style Preferences

- **How you absorb new concepts best:** Step-by-step breakdowns with real-world analogies, building from fundamentals up

- **When you hit confusion:** Prefer to resolve it immediately with a different analogy or concrete example rather than sitting with ambiguity
- **Comfort with ambiguity:** Medium-low

## Analogy Domain

- **Preference:** Flexible, accuracy-first — choose the analogy that most precisely matches the mechanism. Do not default to any single domain.
- **Soccer:** Removed — do not use soccer analogies. If a soccer example were the only perfect fit, prefer a direct explanation instead.

## Input Preferences — Math (intuition-first)

- **Goal:** Build a *playable mental model* of each idea — what it is for, what each piece of its formula does as a knob, how it behaves at the extremes, and when it lies to you. Derivation fluency is explicitly **not** the goal.
- **Workflow:** Formulas are shown openly on screen and read term by term (what grows, what shrinks, what cancels, what happens as a term → 0 or → ∞). The learner restates the *mechanism* in their own words. No multi-step algebraic derivation and no numeric hand-computation drills.
- **Never** require a proof, a derivation from scratch, or a numeric final answer as the proof of understanding.
- **Validation:** Understanding is demonstrated by "say it, place it, spot it break" — explain the mechanism, say when to reach for it and what you'd wrongly reach for instead, and name a regime where it fails. Tiny toy cases (2×2, $N=2$, 3 points) are intuition pumps, not graded work.
- **Still non-negotiable:** precision of the *causal claim*. A vague buzzword ("it captures the variance") without saying how or which way is not an answer.
