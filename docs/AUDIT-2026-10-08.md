## 1. Executive Verdict

**This is an unusually serious, well-engineered learning system — and it is currently optimizing the wrong thing.**

The engineering is real: deterministic SRS arithmetic, receipt-gated emission, contract-bound invariants with named passing tests (`contracts/learning-core.json`), golden tests, drift audits, raw-source immutability, contradiction-surfacing protocols. I know few AI-tutor designs with this much rigor.

But judged by your ultimate test — *does the human become more capable?* — the system has three structural defects:

1. **It verifies conversations, not knowledge.** The gate guarantees "a verifier looked at this text" (receipt binding by token overlap), not "this is true." Claim enumeration is **self-selected by the generator** (`fact-check.md:17` — "if `rendered_content` makes load-bearing claims NOT listed in `claims[]`, flag them" is a *prompt instruction*, not a code check). The architecture can produce `fact-check PASS 4/4` tallies that mean "4 of 4 *listed* claims passed."
2. **The AI does the consolidation of understanding.** The elicit→attempt→consolidate ladder is genuinely good pedagogy, but the Consolidate rung is a tutor-authored expert exposition, "just tell me" is *always honored with no pushback* (`learning-teach/SKILL.md:233`), repair is told after 2 hints, and all durable knowledge (wiki) is Clerk-authored. The learner's remaining cognitive work is largely *confirming* the AI's synthesis.
3. **Nothing in the system ever measures what the learner can do without it.** A grep across both repos for `ai-free|unaided|closed-book|without assistance` returns **zero hits**. Mastery is recency-weighted quiz correctness (`ops.py:239-252`). After 1,000 hours the system cannot distinguish outcome A (independent capability) from outcome B (AI-consulting skill), because it never tests independence.

Add one epistemic inversion: the wiki (AI-synthesized) is declared **Trusted**, while raw sources (primary evidence) are declared **Verify before acting on** (`learning-system/AGENTS.md:230-235`). Trust flows the wrong way.

**Verdict:** a high-quality *verified tutoring delivery system* with strong software engineering discipline, not yet a system that builds independent cognitive capability. The gap is fixable, and the fix is mostly not "add more verification" — it is *move the thinking back to the learner* and *make knowledge provenance real*.

### 4.2 The core structural finding

> **"The AI checked itself" is accurate. Independent verification, in the epistemically meaningful sense, does not exist in this architecture.**

What exists:
- **Process independence:** generator ≠ verifier *role*, different model strings (`glm-5.3-flash` vs `muse-spark-1.3-contributor` vs judge `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`), read-only verifier tools.
- **No epistemic independence:** all are LLMs sharing failure modes; verifier validity is a regex on `"verdict":"PASS"`; the judge sees only what the verifier claims; provider-failure fallback *swaps* verifier models with the scout/clerk family (`primitives.ts:59-60`); OpenWebUI layer has *no* model separation at all (`OPENWEBUI.md:43`: "Tutor's verifier is Tutor's model").
- **Source grounding exists and is real** (verifier must read `raw_files` from disk), which is the single strongest anti-hallucination mechanism in the system. But: the generator chooses the `claims[]`, chooses the `reference_excerpt`, and the gate binds *text coverage*, not *claim completeness*.

### 4.3 What the system guarantees vs. hopes

**Guarantees:** some named verifier produced a PASS-shaped JSON over text that token-overlaps ≥85% with what the learner sees; grades shown were "agreed" by a grade-audit; state writes at review/ingest were audited by a second agent against files on disk; scheduling arithmetic is deterministic.

**Hopes:** that the verifier read the sources carefully; that `claims[]` was complete; that PASS means supported; that evidence reflects work; that "grade-audit agreed" means the grade is right; that the wiki is true.

**Places the system looks safer than it is:**
1. `fact-check PASS 4/4` tallies in session notes — no claim-level JSON is persisted (only `review`-gate JSONs exist in `Reviews/Quality Gates/`).
2. `⛔ UNVERIFIED` banner + content still delivered (`engine.ts:854-858`).
3. `⚠️ REVIEW FLAGS SURFACED` banner + content still delivered.
4. `Knowledge Wiki/wiki/*` = "Trusted" (`AGENTS.md:230-235`).
5. Multi-pass quality gates that terminate in *human fiat* (`pass3-parent-recheck`, `pass4-closing` — honestly labeled, but the gate itself does not converge).
6. `audit_gates.py` runs weekly to a log with `exit 0 always` (`audit-recent-sessions.sh:5-7`) — "not a gate."

### 4.4 Verdict on "as close to zero as practically possible"

With this architecture, the realistic floor is **low-single-digit percent** of load-bearing claims being wrong, dominated by paths 2, 3, 6, 8, 9. Getting materially lower requires:
- deterministic claim extraction (so the generator cannot curate its own exam),
- exact-draft binding (hash the emitted bytes, not bag-of-words coverage),
- per-claim provenance that survives into the wiki,
- and a small **human/primary-source adjudication tier** for load-bearing claims (definitions, formulas, causal mechanisms). Do not pretend a third LLM makes it zero — it doesn't.

## 10. Architecture Audit

### What is structurally right
- **Scout (gather) / Tutor (teach) / Clerk (persist) decomposition** — correct separation of concerns; keep it.
- **Reviewer never writes state; review-clerk writes; review-session-audit checks** — good.
- **Deterministic SRS + queue in `ops.py`** — correct: arithmetic must never be an LLM decision.
- **CONTRACT.md pattern** (invariants with IDs bound to named passing tests, `learn-check` fails if an invariant lacks a test; "update door may never touch contracts/") — this is exemplary. Keep as the model for all future enforcement.
- **Raw-source immutability + hashing + contradiction protocol** — right instincts.

### What is structurally wrong
1. **Verification is content-token-overlap, not content-identity.** Binding should be a hash of the exact emitted draft (or byte-exact copy requirement), not `coverage ≥ 0.85` bag-of-words (`primitives.ts:412-448`). Empty binding must never "bind anything."
2. **Claim enumeration is self-selected.** The generator chooses `claims[]`; completeness is a prompted hope. Fix: deterministic claim extraction (numbers, formulas, definitions, causal assertions) run in code over the draft; the verifier must see *that* list.
3. **Trust is positional, not epistemic.** "Stored in wiki = trusted" is backwards.
4. **Verification and teaching share the same epistemic channel.** A single LLM family produces both the lesson and the fact-check context (source excerpt selection is also the generator's).
5. **Mastery lives in the wrong shape** (scalar activity score), so every downstream decision (scheduling, solid/fuzzy, advancement) is mis-specified.
6. **No independence layer at all** — no AI-free mode, no help-usage telemetry, no fading.
7. **Skill-copy drift is real** between `Skills/` (Open WebUI) and `.pi/skills/` (pi) — review pipelines differ materially (Clerk runs review vs. review-scout/review-clerk split). Two harnesses, two pedagogies. Pick one runtime as canonical; the other is legacy.
8. **State surgery via gitignored `.tmp/gen_review_*.py`** is how corrections actually happen — post-hoc rewriting outside any gate.

### Should some LLM decisions become deterministic? Yes:
- MCQ grading given `correct_index` (code, not grade-audit)
- `q_type` enum enforcement (currently any string accepted, `ops.py:720-741`)
- Concept-type ladder selection & drift detection (already surfaced; should be enforced)
- Claim extraction from drafts (see above)
- Receipt binding (hash)
- Attempts/Active Concepts row schema validation

### Should some deterministic mechanisms become adaptive? Yes:
- Interval ladders should respond to retrieval *effort/confidence*, not correctness alone
- Hint budgets should shrink as independence rises

### Should the system sometimes refuse to teach? **Yes.** When the prerequisite chain shows a broken foundation (Concept B failing due to Concept A), it should refuse to advance and re-derive A first. When load-bearing claims lack sources, it should teach the *question*, not the answer ("here is what the sources say, here is what is unsettled").

## 13. Ideal Architecture

```
                    ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
                    │  EPISTEMIC LAYER (what is true)          │
                    │  sources (immutable, hashed)             │
                    │   → candidate claims (extracted by CODE) │
                    │   → verified claims (source-quoted,      │
                    │     claim-level verdicts persisted)      │
                    │   → contested / unresolved (explicit)    │
                    │   → retracted (propagates to dependents) │
                    │  Trust: source > verified > synthesis*   │
                    │  (*labeled, never silently reused)       │
                    └─────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                      │ grounded context only
                                      ▼
┌──────────────────────┐    ┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│ LEARNER STATE        │◄───│  PEDAGOGICAL LAYER (who thinks) │
│ per concept:         │    │  diagnose → elicit → STRUGGLE   │
│  recall              │    │  window → hint ladder (fading)  │
│  conceptual          │    │  → LEARNER consolidation        │
│  procedural          │    │  → application → transfer probe │
│  transfer            │    │  → delayed AI-free check        │
│  independence        │    │  AI may withhold, question,     │
│  stability           │    │  refuse; may not answer first   │
│  confidence calib.   │    └─────────────────────────────────┘
│  prereq edges        │                     │
└──────────────────────┘                     ▼
        ▲                          ┌─────────────────────────┐
        │   deterministic ops      │  DETERMINISTIC CORE     │
        └──────────────────────────│  SRS math, queue, claim │
                                   │  extraction, MCQ grade, │
                                   │  receipt hashing, gates │
                                   └─────────────────────────┘
```

Principles: (1) AI verifies, learner authors; (2) code decides arithmetic and mechanics, LLM judges only what needs judgment; (3) every persistent claim carries provenance; (4) mastery requires demonstrated independence; (5) the system measures its own failure rate (false-pass/false-block) as a first-class metric.

## 14. Ideal Learning Loop (concept → mastery → retention)

1. **Encounter**: system checks prereq edges; if a prereq is `fuzzy`, it refuses to advance and re-derives the prereq first.
2. **Diagnose**: probe with *learner-generated* predictions ("what do you think happens and why?") before any MCQ recognition crutch; confidence tag stored.
3. **Elicit**: learner states their current model in their own words (free generation, not selection).
4. **Struggle window**: explicit — "work on this for N minutes on paper before you ask." Hints only after the window; hint 1 = a question, hint 2 = method (never answer); hint usage logged as an independence debit.
5. **Learner consolidation**: *the learner* writes the clean statement of the idea (1–3 sentences) in their own words; the AI checks it against verified claims and points out only what's missing or wrong. (This replaces tutor consolidation.)
6. **Consolidate**: AI adds source framing / external angle / contradictions — as *labeled additions to the learner's text*, not replacements.
7. **Apply**: one near-transfer computation on paper.
8. **Transfer probe** (later, spaced): a far-transfer or novel-context item; failure routes to the misconception ledger, not just a retry.
9. **Record**: attempts store correctness, confidence, hint count, question type, independence level; mastery updated on multi-dimensional model.
10. **Schedule**: deterministic SRS, but intervals respond to independence and confidence, not correctness alone.
11. **Delayed AI-free check** (per §21): at escalating intervals, a closed-book, no-hint, no-fact-check test of the concept; its result carries *veto power* over mastery claims.
12. **Knowledge write**: the learner's own concept note (or a marked learner section of the wiki page) becomes the durable artifact; Clerk *verifies and indexes*, never ghost-writes.

## 15. Learner ↔ AI Division of Labor

**The learner should usually do:**
- Predicting, attempting, computing (paper), recalling, explaining in own words, consolidating, diagnosing their own errors, writing concept notes, deciding what feels shaky, checking the AI's claims against sources when asked.

**The AI should usually do:**
- Gathering and hashing sources; surfacing contradictions; scheduling; sequencing prerequisites; generating *fresh* practice instances and counterexamples; grading mechanical work deterministically; flagging where the learner's model breaks; providing detectors ("what to check next time"); retrieving what is due; bookkeeping.

**The AI should never do without learner involvement:**
- Producing the learner's own explanation of an idea; writing the learner's durable understanding; solving the learner's assigned problem; deciding that the learner "knows" something; silently resolving source contradictions.

**The AI should sometimes deliberately refuse to:**
- Answer immediately (after hint budget spent / within struggle window); teach downstream material over a fuzzy prerequisite; assert load-bearing claims with no source ("here's what I don't know"); declare mastery without an independence check; keep teaching when the learner is in answer-grabbing mode ("just tell me" ×3 in a row → name the pattern).

**The system should automate:**
- Scheduling, queue construction, attempt logging, MCQ grading, receipt binding, state audits, provenance bookkeeping, drift detection, review reminders.

**The system should deliberately leave difficult:**
- Cold recall, generation of explanations, far-transfer application, error diagnosis, working through ambiguity, reading the primary source itself (some items should require the learner to open `raw/sources` and find the answer).

## 16. Mastery Model

**Mastery = demonstrated capability across dimensions, with independence as a gate, not a bonus.**

Per concept, track six dimensions (0–3 each):

| Dimension | Evidence required |
|---|---|
| **Recall** | Cold free-recall at ≥2 spaced delays, confidence `sure` |
| **Conceptual** | Explain-back in own words (Feynman rubric: what / when-why / nearest-neighbor / example) — **mandatory**, not optional |
| **Procedural** | Correct execution on fresh instances (paper), no hints |
| **Transfer** | ≥1 novel-context or far-transfer item passed *after* a delay |
| **Independence** | Passed at least one AI-free (closed-book, no-hint) check; hint debt below threshold |
| **Stability** | Performance at 30/60/90 days; no regressions without re-graduation |

**Graduation rules (deterministic, computed in `ops.py`):**
- `developing` → `solid` only when: Recall ≥2 **AND** Conceptual ≥2 **AND** Independence ≥2 **AND** no open misconception **AND** Stability window survived.
- `solid` → `consolidated` (archive) only when: Transfer ≥2 **AND** a 90-day AI-free check passed.
- Any AI-free failure demotes one full level regardless of quiz history (this is the anti-illusion-of-competence veto).
- Confidence calibration tracked separately: high-confidence wrong answers create mandatory misconception rows; low-confidence correct answers do not count as mastery (already implied by `sure/hunch` rules — make it stick).

**Question-type taxonomy enforced in code** (enum, not free strings): `definitional | discriminative | computational | free-recall | transfer-near | transfer-far | explain-back | error-detect`. Mastery dimensions are fed by specific types (transfer-near/far feed Transfer; free-recall feeds Recall; explain-back feeds Conceptual; error-detect feeds Transfer+Metacognition).

## 17. Evaluation & Benchmarking Plan

### Factual reliability
- **Hallucination rate:** monthly sample of 50 load-bearing claims from wiki + recent lessons; adjudicate against primary sources (human, or independent family + mandatory source quote). Metric: % unsupported. Target: <1% load-bearing.
- **False verification rate:** inject 20 seeded errors into sandboxed `/teach` sessions (wrong formula, wrong attribution, wrong number); measure % that pass fact-check. This is your most important reliability experiment — run it before trusting any gate change.
- **False block rate:** seed 20 correct drafts; measure % withheld.
- **Source-grounding rate:** % of claim-level verdicts with a verbatim source quote attached (enforceable in schema).

### Learning
- **Delayed retention:** surprise cold tests at 7/30/90 days on `solid`-tagged concepts. Metric: unaided accuracy. Compare against pre-system baseline (use the archived SWE era as historical control).
- **Transfer:** blind-scored novel problems (e.g., apply PCA thinking to a domain never seen in lessons) monthly.
- **Misconception persistence:** count of misconceptions re-opened after "graduation" (should → 0).
- **Conceptual depth:** blind rating of learner's own concept notes (rubric: mechanism, boundary conditions, nearest-neighbor distinction) before vs. after N months.

### Cognitive health (the ones that decide A vs. B)
- **AI-free performance trend:** % correct on `/solo` checks per month — must *rise*.
- **Hint debt per problem:** hints used per item — must *fall* with exposure.
- **"Just tell me" frequency** — track; must *fall*.
- **Learner-generated ratio:** learner words vs. AI words per session (scriptable on session transcripts) — learner share must rise.
- **Recall-before-explanation rate:** % of minis where the learner's elicit attempt preceded any exposition (currently honored by design; verify in transcripts).

### System quality
- Gate false-pass/false-block (above); state integrity (`audit_state.py` errors/week → 0); runtime/repo drift (versions.lock violations → 0); verification coverage (% of claims turns with persisted claim-level receipts → 100%).

### Concrete experiments
1. **Red-team fortnight** (seeded errors, measure gate catch rate).
2. **A/B on consolidation mode** (tutor-consolidate vs. learner-consolidate) on matched topics; measure 30-day cold recall.
3. **Independence probe**: give 5 problems "without the system"; measure completion vs. system-assisted peers of matched content.
4. **Wiki trust audit**: 30 randomly sampled wiki claims, checked against raw sources; measure error + provenance completeness.

## 18. AI-Free Test Design (`/solo`)

- **When:** every 4th review session becomes a solo session; plus mandatory solo check before any `solid`→`consolidated` graduation; plus a monthly "exam" across tracks.
- **What:** free-recall + one computation + one transfer item per due concept, drawn from the same queue mechanism (`ops.py queue`) but with `mode: solo`. No hints, no fact-check, no viz, no "just tell me." The AI's only role is *grading afterwards* (deterministic where possible) and recording.
- **Mechanics:** learner works entirely on paper/own editor; submits final answers in one message; gate runs in `[[FLOW:solo]]` mode where the tutor is *forbidden* to emit `claims` turns at all (engine-level: block all hints/exposition in solo flow — a genuinely enforceable, simple rule).
- **Results influence:** solo pass → full mastery credit on Independence + Stability; solo fail → mandatory dimension demotion, misconception row if the error is structural, re-teach scheduled regardless of prior quiz history. Solo scores are shown as the headline mastery number; quiz scores become secondary evidence.
- **Dependency detection:** a concept with quiz-pass + solo-fail is a *dependency alarm*; a learner with rising "just tell me" + falling solo scores gets a named intervention ("your unaided accuracy fell; we're re-deriving X without me first").

# Implementation Plan: P0.1 (pi runtime only)

## Goal

Move the **Consolidate** rung from tutor-authored exposition to **learner-generated understanding that the AI checks and extends**, and turn "just tell me" from a silent dependency path into a **logged, compensated** event — without breaking the gate (zero engine changes needed) and without violating "opt-outs are honored, no pushback."

## Design

### New ladder shape (per mini-checkpoint)

```
1. Elicit        (unchanged)  [[TURN:quiz]] purpose:probe, ungraded
2. Attempt       (unchanged)  ≤2 guiding questions, [[TURN:claims]], hint = method only
3a. Learner consolidation   (NEW, default)  [[TURN:none]] procedural prompt:
       "your turn — in one or two lines, what's the idea here, in your words?"
3b. Check + extend          (CHANGED)  [[TURN:claims]], fact-checked:
       check learner's statement vs verified content → quote what's right in
       their words, name what's missing/wrong → add ONLY what they didn't
       produce, labeled ("Source framing:", "External angle:", "⚠️ Sources disagree…")
```

Rules:
- **3a is the default**; skip it only for (i) pure notation / mechanical micro-steps with no conceptual payload, or (ii) explicit learner opt-out.
- **3b never replaces the learner's phrasing** with a polished substitute; it extends it.
- If the 3a prompt embeds substantive framing (not just "your turn"), it becomes `[[TURN:claims]]` and gets fact-checked (consistent with existing hint/question rules).
- **Compression for pace:** may batch the 3a request across at most 2 adjacent minis when the learner signals pace pressure; never leave >2 consecutive minis with no learner generation rung.

### "Just tell me" (kept honored, made visible)

- Jump straight to 3b content with **no pushback** (no "are you sure", no argument) — behavior preserved.
- The mini still closes with **one minimal generation beat**: one line ("before we move on: when does this apply?") or the already-scheduled isomorphic micro-check. This is the mini's normal closing beat, not pushback.
- If the learner declines that too → move on silently, log `declined_generation`.

### Error-repair close (line 221)

After a told repair: learner restates the corrected point in one line (or the scheduled isomorphic micro-check). Told repairs log `told_repair`.

### Dependency event logging (countable, no `ops.py` schema change)

Schema: `{kind: "just_tell_me"|"told_repair"|"declined_generation", mini: "CP3.2", date, note?}`

Written at handoff to:
1. **Session note** — `## Dependency events` section (0 or listed).
2. **`Core/Pending Ingest.json`** — optional `dependency_events: [...]` field (additive; `tutor-audit` only checks required fields, so nothing breaks).

# Audit summary

## What I did

I read both repos as one system. I checked code, prompts, state files, and real session records. I did not trust the READMEs.

## The verdict

The engineering is strong. The target is wrong. The system makes good chats. It does not yet prove that the Learner grows.

## What is good

- **Process.** `ops.py` does the schedule math in code. It never lets an LLM choose a date.
- **Interaction.** The loop is Probe first, explain second. The Learner recalls on paper. Feedback comes after the answer, not before.
- **Repair.** The Tutor names the slip and gives a detector: "what to check next time".
- **State.** `Mistakes.md` keeps each error with the Learner's own words.
- **Contract.** Each gate rule has a named test. A change without a test fails the check.

## The three flaws

1. **Interaction.** The end of each mini-checkpoint is the Tutor's clean statement. The Learner accepts it. The Learner does not make his own words.
2. **Computation.** The gate checks word overlap. The Tutor picks the claims to check. A wrong claim can stay outside the list. The receipt looks safe. It is not safe.
3. **State.** `Attempts.json` stores dates and pass/fail. It does not store confidence, help used, or unaided skill. The system never tests the Learner with no AI. So we cannot see if he grows.

## Where false facts get in

- The Tutor writes the claim list. The checker only sees that list.
- When sources are quiet, the checker uses model memory.
- The Knowledge Wiki holds AI text as `Trusted`. The raw sources sit below it. Trust runs the wrong way.
- Two models share one failure mode. A second model is not a second truth.
- Fact-check runs on the same path as the teach. It is a self-check, not an outside check.

## Where the AI does the Learner's work

- The Tutor states each idea at the end.
- "Just tell me" is always obeyed, with no cost and no log.
- After two hints, the Tutor tells the fix.
- The Clerk writes all wiki pages. The Learner writes none.
- The last review records show `feynman: none`. The rule says the Feynman pass is required. Practice skips it.

## Mastery now vs. mastery needed

**Now:** two late correct answers, a longer date, and no open mistake. Then the tag is `solid`. The number is advice only. All 40 live Concepts sit at `developing`. None is `consolidated`.

**Needed:** six parts — recall, own words, procedure, transfer, unaided skill, and stability at 30/60/90 days. A fail with no AI must drop the tag. One quiz score must never grant it.

## What the system cannot tell us

After 1,000 hours it cannot show: (A) the Learner thinks better alone, or (B) the Learner asks AI better. No test exists for this. This is the one gap that decides the whole goal.

## The fix order

1. Learner states each idea. Log "just tell me". (Planned. Not started.)
2. Code picks the claims. The receipt binds the exact text.
3. Save claim verdicts as JSON.
4. `/solo` test: closed book, no AI help.
5. Rework mastery to count unaided skill.
6. Add provenance to the Knowledge Wiki.

Each fix is small. The order matters. Step 1 and step 4 change the target. The rest make the target safe.
